Manifolds in Machine Learning

Mini-course notes · Machine Learning · Geometry · Work in progress

These notes introduce the idea of manifolds and explain why they are useful in machine learning, especially for representation learning, dimensionality reduction, geometric deep learning, and anomaly detection.

1. Motivation

Modern machine learning often deals with high-dimensional data: images, signals, biological measurements, industrial sensor streams, and many other types of observations. However, the meaningful degrees of freedom may be much smaller than the ambient dimension.

Main idea: data can be high-dimensional in its raw representation while having a lower-dimensional intrinsic structure. Learning this structure is often more useful than treating observations as arbitrary points.

2. What is a manifold?

Informally, a manifold is a space that may be globally curved or complex, but that locally looks like a Euclidean space. A simple example is the surface of the Earth: locally it looks flat and two-dimensional, although it is embedded in three-dimensional space.

3. The manifold hypothesis

The manifold hypothesis states that many real-world datasets concentrate near lower-dimensional structures. This idea motivates dimensionality reduction, representation learning, autoencoders, and several geometric approaches to machine learning.

4. Links with anomaly detection

In anomaly detection, normal observations may lie close to a learned manifold or low-dimensional structure. Observations that deviate from this structure can be interpreted as potential anomalies, depending on the application and the scoring rule.
